# Claim-Evidence Assessment: Experiment 01

## Status criteria

| Status | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence available | A documented diagnostic or design fact directly addresses the specified synthetic-evaluation question. This category is retained for future use but is not assigned in this preliminary assessment. It would not establish that evidence is sufficient for the intended claim. |
| Documented limitation | A diagnostic or design fact explicitly identifies a scope, coverage, sampling, or generalization constraint. |
| Partial evidence | Relevant evidence exists but is limited in scope, representativeness, independence, or realism for the specified question. |
| Not assessed | The profile has no diagnostic or collected check for the specified question. This is not evidence of poor performance. |

These preliminary statuses reflect evidence relative to the intended claim, not numerical levels of validity. They describe available information, not overall validity, and do not combine into a score, certification, or deployment decision.

## Assessment basis

| Evidence dimension | A: Concentrated | B: Broader | C: Broader with scoring checks | Explicit basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Construct representation | Documented limitation | Partial evidence | Partial evidence | A has zero E1 and E2 scenarios. B/C document all six prespecified observable indicators and their construct and dimension map, but the taxonomy is illustrative and unvalidated. |
| Scenario allocation and diversity | Documented limitation | Partial evidence | Partial evidence | A has highly concentrated primary-indicator counts and 10 families. B/C allocate scenarios across all six indicators and document 48 families, but counts and family labels do not establish real-use representativeness or adequate construct coverage. |
| Scoring repeatability | Not assessed | Not assessed | Partial evidence | A/B have one primary label per scenario. C has a 60-scenario simulated repeat-label check with intentional failure oversampling and controlled disagreement. |
| Simulated reference agreement | Not assessed | Not assessed | Partial evidence | A/B have no reference labels. C has simulated human-reference labels only, not ratings from people; the comparison does not establish human alignment. |
| Generalizability | Documented limitation | Documented limitation | Documented limitation | All profiles use fictional, clustered, non-random synthetic scenarios and one hypothetical system configuration. |
| Statistical uncertainty | Not assessed | Not assessed | Not assessed | No statistical-uncertainty estimate is collected for any profile. Family clustering and constructed sampling mean that naive random-sample confidence intervals would be inappropriate for a real population. |

## Warranted interpretations

| Profile | Narrowly supported descriptive statement | Broader inference that remains unjustified | Evidence needed next |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | The synthetic primary judge recorded 190 passes out of 200 constructed scenarios under the documented configuration. | That the hypothetical assistant adequately avoids the full Psychological Harm construct for adult coaching use. | Representation of omitted indicators, broader scenario coverage, independent scoring checks, and evidence connected to realistic use conditions. |
| B | The synthetic primary judge recorded 190 passes out of 200 broader constructed scenarios, with all six prespecified indicators represented. | That balanced counts or more families establish sufficient coverage, valid scoring, or safety in use. | Justified sampling from relevant conditions, review of taxonomy and family labels, and independent scoring evidence. |
| C | The synthetic primary judge recorded 190 passes out of 200 broader constructed scenarios, and the selected subset has documented simulated label comparisons. | That simulated agreement establishes real scorer reliability, validity, or production safety. | Actual independent human review, a justified sampling design, scoring validation, and evidence about relevant use conditions. |

All materials and outcomes in this experiment are synthetic. Evidence availability is not evidence sufficiency.
